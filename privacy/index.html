Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 November 2026

Kelo Cam is a camera and photo-editing app. This policy explains what happens to your data when you use it. It is written in plain English; if something here is unclear, ask us using the contact line at the end.

Your photos stay on your iPhone

Photos you take are stored inside the app on your iPhone. The camera analyses the live picture (scene, faces and framing) on your iPhone; nothing the camera sees is sent anywhere. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence turned on, the AI Framing card can also ask Apple's on-device model for a framing tip about the current picture; that also happens on your iPhone, and nothing is sent. When you export a photo, the app adds it to your photo library. The app can add photos to your library, and it can read only the photos you pick yourself in the system photo picker (and the photos in its own library). It cannot browse the rest of your photo library. A photo leaves your iPhone only when you use an AI feature, as described below, or ask Siri about a photo, as described next. When you ask Siri to change a Kelo Cam photo, Apple Intelligence receives that photo as it currently looks (without location) and a few facts about it (date, kind, current edit settings) so it knows which photo you mean; Apple handles it under its own privacy terms, on your iPhone or on Apple's Private Cloud Compute. The change itself is made by the app on your iPhone.

Your choices

AI features need your agreement. The first time you use one, the app shows a consent sheet that names the provider and the place where your photo is processed, and says what is sent. Nothing is sent to the service until you tap "Agree — I'm 18 or older", and that includes the signed subscription record described below; if you tap "Not now" or "I'm under 18", nothing is sent and the rest of the app works as before. You can withdraw your agreement at any time in Me → AI and privacy; the app then asks again before the next AI action. Sending a place is a second, separate choice that is asked only for the Travel Map style (which AI One-Tap also uses for landscape photos), the first time a place would be read from a photo or from the camera's record. If you say no, you can type a place yourself instead. Withdrawing does not affect anything done before.

AI edits (Pro)

On iPhones with Apple Intelligence turned on, the chat box's edits are worked out by Apple's on-device model on your iPhone: the photo and your words are not sent anywhere, and these edits do not count towards your monthly AI edits. On other iPhones the chat box uses our AI service, as described below.

AI features are part of the Pro subscription and are only for people aged 18 or over; the app asks you to confirm your age before the first AI edit, and if you say you are under 18 nothing is sent. When you ask for an AI edit, the app sends our AI service a reduced copy of the photo, re-encoded without location or camera metadata, together with the words you type (an instruction in a chat box, or the description of a style you write yourself) and your current edit settings. The photo may show faces. The service uses them only to carry out the edit and sends the result back. The words you type are sent to the service and on to the AI provider together with the photo. Nothing is sent unless you start an AI edit and have agreed to it.

The app also sends Apple's signed App Store transaction record for your Pro subscription with every AI request, so that the service can check that you are a subscriber. According to Apple's documentation, the record includes transaction identifiers, dates, the product, the price and currency, the app identifier and the store country, and it contains no name, email or payment details. The Me tab also asks the service how many AI edits you have left this month, and sends the same record to do so, but only after you have agreed to AI processing.

Our service does not store your photo in its own records. It holds the photo in memory while the edit runs, and it never writes a photo, an instruction or a result to its logs. The platform that runs the service may make temporary technical copies in memory while it handles a request; they are not written to disk. To make the edit, the service passes the request on to our AI provider, Google Cloud (Vertex AI), which processes it for us as our processor. Google does not use the photo, your words or the result to train or improve its models without our permission, and we have not given it. We have turned off Vertex AI's short-term response cache. Google may log requests for a limited time to detect abuse of its services, and its documentation does not say for how long, so we make no promise that our provider keeps nothing.

AI One-Tap on a landscape photo is the Travel Map style. Like that style, it sends the place only after you allow it separately (see Your choices). The place is the place name and coordinates rounded to about 1 km, or only the name of a place you picked yourself, which is sent because you picked it. On a photo of people or pets, AI One-Tap sends no place. The place name can be more specific than the coordinates: it is usually a town or city, but can occasionally be the name of a street or a landmark. The service sends back a new image. If you save it, the app keeps it on your iPhone as a new photo in its library; your original photo is not changed.

When you use an AI style, the app sends a reduced copy of the photo you choose and, for a style you describe yourself, the words you type. The Travel Map style also sends the place: coordinates rounded to about 1 km that the app reads from the photo you picked (or has recorded for a photo in its library), or a place you choose yourself. That place is read and sent only after you agree to it.

Where your data is processed

Our service runs on Google Cloud in the Netherlands (region europe-west4), and its records are stored there. It calls Google's Vertex AI through Vertex AI's EU endpoint, which processes AI requests only within EU member states (not in the United Kingdom or Switzerland).

What our service keeps

The service sees a pseudonymous subscription code. It is calculated from the App Store's original transaction number for your subscription, using a secret key that only the service holds; we never store the transaction number itself. It is not your name, an account or a device identifier. Because it is the same each month and on every device with the same subscription, it counts as personal data, and we treat it that way.

The service uses the code to count your AI edits and attempts, so it can enforce the monthly and daily limits and keep its costs under control. It keeps three kinds of record.

A usage record for each month holds the code, whether the purchase was made in the sandbox or in production, the month, the number of AI edits counted, the number of attempts, random request numbers (with expiry times) of requests that are running or have already been counted, and an expiry time. It is deleted automatically about 90 days after its last update; the deletion can run up to a day late.

A daily attempts record holds the code, the day and the number of attempts that day. It is deleted automatically about 7 days after its last update. A service-wide daily counter holds only a total number of attempts for the day, with no code and no personal data.

Our service's own application log records only a request number, the route, the style, the result status, how long it took, the model used and a shortened form of the pseudonymous code. It never contains a photo, an instruction, words you typed, a place or a result. These application logs are deleted after 30 days.

Separately, the hosting platform (Google Cloud Run) writes its own request log for every request that reaches the service. It records the IP address of the connection, the user agent (a short text that identifies the app and the system software), the time, the route, the status and the size of the request, and it does not contain the content of the request. It is stored in the EU (the Netherlands) and kept for 30 days.

Share codes

Every copy of Kelo Cam has its own share code. If a friend who has just installed the app enters your code, you each get 5 free Plus uses; one use unlocks the Plus features for one photo. Plus uses never remove the watermark and never include the AI edits that use our service.

To run this, the app makes a random identifier the first time it is opened and keeps it in your iPhone's Keychain (it stays if you delete and reinstall the app, not if you erase the iPhone). It is not your name, an account or an advertising identifier; it identifies only this copy of the app, and it is never used for tracking or advertising. Our service stores only a scrambled form of it (a SHA-256 hash), together with your share code, when it was first and last used, whether your iPhone was new to Kelo Cam, the code you entered (if any), how many Plus uses you have earned and how many wrong codes were tried on the most recent day. These records are deleted automatically about 24 months after their last use.

So that one iPhone cannot collect the reward again by reinstalling the app, the app asks Apple's DeviceCheck service for a token, and our service passes it to Apple to read and set two yes-or-no marks that Apple keeps for your iPhone and our app: "Kelo Cam has seen this iPhone" and "this iPhone has entered a share code". The token is not stored, and the marks hold nothing else. How many Plus uses you have left, and which photos they unlocked, is kept only on your iPhone.

AI-generated marking

A photo made by an AI feature is marked as AI-generated in its file data (a machine-readable note in the photo's metadata, saying it was made with a trained algorithm). The mark is kept when the photo is saved in the app's library and when it is exported or shared, including after later edits. There is no visible AI label. The mark contains no information about you.

Separately, photos you save to your photo library may carry a small visible "Kelo Cam" brand mark near the bottom edge. The free version always adds it; with Plus or Pro you can turn it off in Me. It is not an AI label, and it contains no information about you. By default a saved photo also gets a white info frame underneath that shows the camera settings, the date and time and the device it was taken with, read from the photo itself; it never shows where the photo was taken, and you can turn it off in Me. The photos kept in the app are not changed.

Location

If Save photo locations is on (Me → Camera), the app notes the approximate place (about 1 km, or wider if you allow only approximate location) where each photo is taken, and its place name, in the app's library on your iPhone. Location is read only while the camera is open, and it is never written into the photos you export or share. Place names are looked up with Apple's geocoding service. You can turn the setting off or deny location access at any time; AI One-Tap then asks you for the place instead. If you save an AI style result made from a photo you picked, the new photo keeps that photo's approximate location only if Save photo locations is on; if it is off, no location is kept.

Purchases

Subscriptions are sold and managed by Apple. We never see your name or payment details. The signed record described under AI edits (Pro) is the only information about your purchase that reaches our service. The service checks Apple's signature and uses only the product, its expiry, whether it was refunded, whether it is a sandbox or a production purchase, and the original transaction number (to work out the pseudonymous code described above).

No tracking

Kelo Cam has no ads, no analytics, no tracking across apps or websites, and no account. The share-code identifier described above is never used for tracking or advertising.

Data kept on your iPhone

Your edits, recipes, imported looks, photo locations, your consent choices and your Plus uses are stored only on your iPhone. The number of AI edits used this month is shown from the service's count, with the last known figure kept on your iPhone. Deleting the app deletes what is on your iPhone, except the share-code identifier and your Plus uses, which stay in your iPhone's Keychain (so a reinstall does not hand out rewards twice) until the iPhone is erased.

Why we may process this data

Under the EU and UK GDPR we rely on your consent for processing a photo and the words you type for an AI edit, which you can withdraw as described above; on our legitimate interests in providing the Pro subscription and keeping the service within its limits and safe from misuse for the pseudonymous code, the counts and the logs; and on our legitimate interests in providing the share codes you use and keeping them from being misused for the share-code records.

Your rights

Under the EU and UK GDPR you can ask what personal data we hold about you, and ask us to correct or delete it, restrict or object to its use, or receive a copy. The personal data we may hold is the pseudonymous code with its usage and attempts records, the share-code records, our application logs and the hosting platform's request log, all described above. We cannot look it up from your name, so we may ask you for help in finding the right record; the records are deleted after the periods described above anyway. You can withdraw your agreement to AI processing at any time in Me → AI and privacy. You can also complain to your data protection authority.
